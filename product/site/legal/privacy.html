Privacy Policy

Effective 11 August 2026 · Last updated 6 August 2026

Short version: we collect your email address and the answers you type into the builder. We use them to build and send your Experiment Card, and — only if you tick the box — to email you now and then. Your email address and your answers are never sent to our analytics. We do use cookies to measure the site and to run ads, and you choose which ones in the banner. Ask us to delete your data and we will.

The longer version is below, and it's still short, because we genuinely don't collect much. There's no account, no password, and no profile.

1. What we collect

Two things, and that's the whole list:

  • Your email address — if you give it to us. You give it at checkout (so you get a receipt and your access link) and again if you ask us to email your Experiment Card.
  • Your builder answers — the eight answers you type about your business and the decision you're stuck on, plus the card generated from them. These stay in your own browser while you work, and only reach our servers if you ask us to email you the card. Section 3 explains exactly how that works.

We also collect usage data about the site itself — which pages get visited, how far people get through the builder, and where visitors came from. It doesn't include your name or anything you typed, but it isn't strictly anonymous either: some of it is tied to a random ID stored in your browser. Sections 5 and 9 explain it properly.

What we don't collect

  • No name, phone number, postal address, company details or job title. We never ask.
  • No account and no password, because there isn't one to create.
  • No card numbers. We never see them — see section 6.
  • Nothing bought from a data broker, and no profile of you built from your answers.

One thing we used to be able to say and no longer can: our advertising and analytics providers do observe you across other websites, in the ordinary way that ad platforms do. That only happens if you accept those cookies — section 9 is the honest account of it.

2. What we use it for

Your builder answers are used to generate your Experiment Card and to deliver it to you — on screen, and in the email if you ask for one. That's it. We don't publish them, we don't share them, and we don't use your answers as marketing material or examples.

Your email address is used for exactly three things:

  1. Sending your receipt and your access link.
  2. Sending you your Experiment Card, if you ask for it.
  3. Sending you occasional emails about the method and new experiments — only if you explicitly consented by ticking the box. It's a separate, unticked choice, and it is never a condition of getting what you paid for.

Giving us your email is optional. If you'd rather not, the Experiment Card still appears on screen and you can copy or print it. The email is a convenience, never a gate on something you already paid for.

Every marketing email has a working unsubscribe link. One click, no reply needed, no "are you sure". We record which track you chose (getting first traction, or scaling) so what we send is relevant rather than generic.

3. Where your answers live

Your answers are kept in your own browser while you work, so you can close the tab and pick up where you left off on the same device. If you ask us to email you the card, a copy of the card travels with that email and is stored with your email record so we can resend it if you lose it.

Clearing your browser data clears the local copy. If you've asked us to email the card, ask us to delete it and we'll remove our copy too (section 7).

4. We don't sell your email or your answers

We don't sell, rent, or trade your email address or your builder answers. Not to advertisers, not to partners, not to anyone. That is a flat commitment, and it's the part of this policy we care most about keeping.

We do use a small number of service providers to actually run the thing — a payment provider, an email provider, and analytics and advertising tools. Most of them process data purely on our instructions. Advertising platforms are the exception worth naming: when you accept advertising cookies, the platform also uses what it observes for its own purposes under its own privacy policy, and we can't promise otherwise on their behalf. Sharing data with an ad platform to measure and target our own campaigns counts as "sharing" (and, under some US state laws, as a "sale") even though no money changes hands for your data — section 9 tells you how to switch it off.

5. Analytics and advertising

We count things like page views and how far people get through the builder, so we can see where the tool confuses people and fix it. We also measure which ads and channels bring people here, because that's how a small business decides where to spend its next pound.

We use two analytics tools, on purpose and for different jobs:

  • Plausible Analytics — no cookies, no ID stored on your device, nothing that identifies a returning visitor. It runs for everyone, because there is nothing here to consent to. It's what we actually use to make product decisions.
  • Google Analytics — this one does store a random ID in a cookie, so it can tell a returning visit from a new one and follow a visit across pages. We use it for longer-term trends and to measure advertising. It only runs if you accept analytics cookies.

We also run advertising tools from the platforms we buy ads on. Where you allow it, these let us see which ad led to a purchase and show our ads to people who visited but didn't buy — ordinary remarketing. They work by storing an identifier on your device, and the platform may combine what it sees here with what it sees elsewhere, under its own privacy policy.

Your email address is never sent to our analytics or advertising tools. Neither are your builder answers. What they see are events — "someone reached step 4", "someone generated a card" — plus which track was chosen and where the visit came from. They do not receive who you are or what you wrote. That isn't a matter of good intentions: the code that records these events drops anything that looks like an email address and anything that isn't on a fixed list of allowed fields, whatever it's called.

So the honest summary: what you type stays private; how you browse is measured. If you'd rather it weren't, decline in the cookie banner and Plausible alone will count you — anonymously, and with no way back to you.

6. Payment

Payments are processed by Lemon Squeezy, acting as merchant of record. They collect your payment details and whatever billing information tax rules require, under their own privacy policy. We never see or store your full card number.

What reaches us from a purchase is your email address and the fact that you bought.

7. Getting your data removed

You can ask us at any time to delete your email address, your stored answers, or both.

Email contact@lessriskmoregrowth.com from the address you used, and say what you want removed. You don't have to give a reason, and asking has no effect on anything you've already bought.

You can also ask us to:

  • tell you what we hold about you,
  • send you a copy of it,
  • correct it,
  • withdraw your cookie consent (or just change it in the banner — same result, faster), or
  • stop emailing you (or just click unsubscribe — same result, faster).

We'll deal with it within 30 days and confirm by email when it's done. Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.

One honest limitation: if we've already emailed you your Experiment Card, that email is in your inbox and we can't reach into it. Deleting our copy doesn't delete yours.

8. How long we keep things

  • If you consented to emails: we keep your email address until you unsubscribe or ask us to delete it.
  • If you didn't consent: we keep your email only as long as we need it for your receipt, your access link, and any refund window — then it goes.
  • Your builder answers: the copy in your browser lasts until you clear your browser data. If a copy reached us because you asked for the card by email, we keep it for as long as your email address is on our list — and it is deleted together with your email address when you unsubscribe or ask us to delete you.
  • The copy of your card stored with your email: same rule. We keep it so we can resend the card if you lose it, and it goes when your email address goes.
  • Purchase records: Lemon Squeezy keeps what tax and accounting rules require them to keep, on their own schedule.

In short: nothing you typed outlives your email address on our list. Delete the address and the answers and the card go with it.

9. Cookies and local storage

Three kinds of thing get stored in your browser by this site. Only the first is unavoidable.

  • Strictly necessary storage. Your builder answers, kept on your device so you don't lose them if you close the tab, and the record of the choice you made in the cookie banner. No consent needed for these — without them the thing you came for doesn't work. They are never sent anywhere by themselves.
  • Analytics cookies. A Google Analytics cookie holding a random ID, so we can distinguish a returning visit from a new one and see how the site performs over months rather than days. Typically expires within two years, and you can clear it any time from your browser. Declining costs you nothing — Plausible still counts the visit without a cookie, which is why our product decisions don't depend on your answer here.
  • Advertising cookies. Identifiers set by the platforms we advertise on, used to attribute a purchase to the ad that caused it and to show our ads again to people who visited without buying. These are the ones that can follow you to other websites. They are the reason this banner exists.

Analytics and advertising cookies only run if you accept them. Until you choose, they don't fire at all — nothing is set in advance and asked about later. You can change your mind whenever you like from the cookie link in the footer, and withdrawing is exactly as easy as accepting was.

We're being blunt about this because an earlier version of this page said we ran no advertising trackers, and that is no longer true. We'd rather change the sentence than quietly outgrow it. What has not changed is the line in section 5: your email address and your builder answers never reach any of these tools, whatever you click in the banner.
