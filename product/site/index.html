What you get: one Experiment Card

One page, eight fields, filled in from your answers. Copy it, print it, or have it emailed to you.

  1. The bet — the decision you're about to make with real money.
  2. The hypothesis — what you believe, written so it can turn out to be wrong.
  3. The smallest test — the concrete thing you run, step by step.
  4. Budget cap — the most you can lose on it, as a number.
  5. Timebox — the date you start and the date you read the result.
  6. Success metric + threshold — what you count, and the number that counts as a yes.
  7. Kill criteria — the number below which you stop and don't extend the budget.
  8. If it works / if it doesn't — your next move in either case, decided in advance.

An example we made up, so you can see the shape of the output. It's not a customer story — we're not going to show you results that aren't ours.

Say you run a two-person studio building websites for small restaurants, and you're about to spend six weeks packaging a fixed-price audit service. Here's what the card might look like.

1 · The bet
Spend six weeks building a fixed-price $600 "restaurant website audit" and put it on the site.
2 · The hypothesis
I believe restaurant owners I've already worked with would pay $600 for a fixed-scope audit — before I build the package.
3 · The smallest test
The Pre-Sale Page. One page describing exactly what the audit covers, with a real payment button taking a $150 deposit. Email it to the 25 past and current clients I already have addresses for. Nothing gets built until someone pays.
4 · Budget cap
$75 — page builder and email tool for the month. If it costs more than that, I stop and rethink the test, not the budget.
5 · Timebox
Starts Monday 17 August 2026 · Read Thursday 27 August 2026
6 · Success metric + threshold
Paid $150 deposits from the 25 people emailed. At least 3.
7 · Kill criteria
Fewer than 3 deposits by 27 August → stop, refund every deposit that day, and don't build the package. Do not extend the deadline to "give it more time".
8 · If it works / if it doesn't
If it works: build the audit for the people who already paid, and leave the page up. If it doesn't: I've spent $75 and ten days instead of six weeks — and I go ask the people who didn't buy what they'd have paid for instead.
